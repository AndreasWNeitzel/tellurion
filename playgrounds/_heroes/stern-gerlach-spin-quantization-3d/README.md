# Stern-Gerlach Spin Quantization (Hero)

The 1922 experiment that broke classical mechanics. A beam of
neutral atoms goes through an inhomogeneous magnetic field. The
deflection $\Delta z$ on the screen would, classically, smear out
into a continuous band; quantum mechanically it splits into $2J+1$
discrete spots. Stern and Gerlach saw two spots for silver atoms,
proving that angular momentum is quantized.

## What to look for

The 3D apparatus has the silver-atom oven on the left, the magnet
poles in the middle (red N at the top, blue S at the bottom, with
the canonical wedge geometry that produces the field gradient), and
a screen on the right. Atoms stream through, color-coded by their
$m_J$ projection. The right panel histograms hits on the screen and
overlays the theoretical Gaussian-peak prediction (white) or the
classical uniform band (orange dashed); the $m_J$ tick labels mark
the predicted spot positions.

## Controls

The $J$ selector chooses spin-1/2 (the silver-atom case, two spots),
spin-1 (three spots), spin-3/2 (four spots), or spin-2 (five spots).
The gradient slider $\mathrm{d}B/\mathrm{d}z$ scales the spot
spacing. The atom-rate slider controls the firing rate. The mode
selector switches between quantum (discrete spots), classical
(continuous band), or both overlaid for direct comparison: the
visual signature of the classical-vs-quantum prediction shift is
unmistakable.

## Source

Sakurai and Napolitano, *Modern Quantum Mechanics*, 2nd ed., CUP
2017, Section 1.1. Original experiment: Gerlach and Stern, *Z. Phys.*
9 (1922) 349.
