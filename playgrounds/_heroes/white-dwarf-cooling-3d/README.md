# White-Dwarf Cooling Sequence (Hero)

A white dwarf has no fusion. It is supported by electron degeneracy
pressure and slowly radiates the thermal energy of its residual ion
gas. Mestel (1952) showed that the luminosity decays as $L \propto
M\, t^{-7/5}$; the coldest WDs in the Galactic disk are about 9 Gyr
old and that timescale puts a lower bound on the age of the disk.

## What to look for

Four panels: the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram at the top traces the
cooling tracks of four representative WD masses (0.4, 0.6, 0.9, 1.2
$M_\odot$), with the active mass highlighted in gold and the current
$\log L$ vs $\log T_{\rm eff}$ position drawn as a glowing dot
coloured by its photospheric blackbody. The disk-age cutoff at $\sim
9\,\mathrm{Gyr}$ is marked. Below: the WD photosphere as a glowing
sphere (colour shifts from blue-white to deep red as it cools); the
interior cross-section showing the H/He envelope, C/O liquid mantle
and yellow crystallized core; and the crystallization-fraction
timeline $f_X(t)$ with the current age marked.

## Controls

The mass slider sets $M$ from 0.40 to 1.20 $M_\odot$ (more-massive WDs
are smaller because $R \propto \sqrt{(M_{\rm Ch}/M)^{2/3} -
(M/M_{\rm Ch})^{2/3}}$ has a strict upper bound at $M_{\rm Ch} =
1.44 M_\odot$). The log-age slider sweeps from $10^7$ yr to
$10^{10.2}$ yr. The animation speed slider auto-sweeps the age.

Set $M = 0.6$ and watch the WD slide from $\log L = 0$ at 10 Myr down
to $\log L = -4.5$ at 10 Gyr; the crystal core grows from $f_X = 0$
to $f_X \approx 1$ as $\Gamma$ crosses 175 in the C/O plasma.
