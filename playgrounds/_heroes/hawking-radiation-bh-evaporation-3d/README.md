# Hawking Radiation and BH Evaporation (Hero)

In 1974 Hawking showed that black holes are not black. Quantum field
theory on the curved Schwarzschild background predicts a thermal flux
at temperature $T_H = \hbar c^3 / (8\pi G M k_B) \propto 1/M$.
Bigger BHs are colder; smaller ones are hotter and evaporate faster.
A solar-mass BH lasts $10^{67}$ yr; primordial $10^{11}\,\mathrm{kg}$
BHs from the early universe are evaporating now.

## What to look for

A Schwarzschild horizon (black disk) ringed by an orange photon glow.
Particle-antiparticle pairs pop into existence at the horizon, drawn
as bright white-yellow flashes; the in-falling negative-energy mode
leaves a short streak into the BH, and the outgoing positive-energy
quantum drifts away from the horizon as a small blue-white dot
(Hawking radiation). On the right, the evaporation timeline shows
$M(t)$ (cyan, linear) shrinking and $T_H(t)$ (orange, log) climbing;
a dashed marker follows the current time.

## Controls

The $\log_{10} M$ slider sets the initial BH mass from 1 kg to a
billion solar masses (the actual physics is identical; only the
numbers change). The timeline-fraction slider sweeps $t / t_{\rm
evap}$ from 0 (fresh BH) to 0.99 (just before the final flash).
The preset menu jumps to canonical scales: primordial BH
($1.7 \times 10^{11}$ kg, evaporates in $\sim$ age of universe),
solar-mass BH, or toy kilogram-scale.

## Source
