# Rayleigh-Taylor Instability (Hero)

A dense fluid above a lighter one in a gravity field is unstable.
The interface grows perturbations exponentially at the rate $\sigma =
\sqrt{A k g}$ (Rayleigh 1883; Taylor 1950), where $A$ is the Atwood
number, and saturates into the characteristic spike-and-bubble
pattern. The mushroom-shaped fingers of the Crab Nebula, jellyfish
galaxies, and nuclear-blast clouds are RT-driven.

## What to look for

A horizontal interface separates a heavy (red) fluid on top from a
lighter (cyan) one on bottom. Gravity points down. A small
sinusoidal perturbation grows: red parcels fall in narrow spikes;
cyan parcels rise as wider bubbles. The label at the centerline
tracks the regime (linear, transitional, nonlinear).

## Controls

- Atwood number $A$ in $[-0.5, 0.95]$. Negative $A$ (light on top) is
  a stable density inversion; the interface oscillates instead of
  growing.
- Wavenumber $k$ (1 to 6 modes per box width).
- Gravity $g$.
- Animation speed.
