# pp Chain vs CNO Cycle (Hero)

A side-by-side view of the two hydrogen-burning networks that power
main-sequence stars. The pp chain produces a helium-4 nucleus by
slowly fusing four protons; the CNO cycle does the same net reaction
but with carbon, nitrogen and oxygen acting as catalysts that come
back unchanged at the end. Both deliver $Q = 26.73$ MeV per helium
nucleus made.

## What to look for

The left panel draws the pp chain as three vertical rows: two protons
fuse to a deuteron with a positron and a neutrino, the deuteron picks
up a third proton to make $^3{\rm He}$, then two $^3{\rm He}$ make
$^4{\rm He}$ and free two protons. The right panel draws the CNO
cycle as a closed hexagon: $^{12}{\rm C} \to ^{13}{\rm N} \to ^{13}{\rm
C} \to ^{14}{\rm N} \to ^{15}{\rm O} \to ^{15}{\rm N} \to ^{12}{\rm
C}$. Arrows brighten when that branch is dominant at the current
temperature. The bottom-left bar shows the pp/CNO fractional split at
the chosen $T$, and the bottom-right plot shows $\log
\varepsilon_{\rm pp}(T) \propto T^4$ vs $\log \varepsilon_{\rm CNO}(T)
\propto T^{17}$, with the cross-over temperature marked.

## Controls

Slide the core temperature $T_7$ (units of $10^7$ K) from M-dwarf
cores ($T_7 \approx 0.8$, all pp) up to O-star cores ($T_7 \approx
3.5$, all CNO). Use the preset menu to jump between an M dwarf, the
Sun, an F star, an A star and an O star. The animation-speed slider
controls how fast the arrows pulse.
