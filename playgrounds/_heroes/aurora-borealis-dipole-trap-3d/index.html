Aurora Borealis - Charged Particles in Earth's Dipole

What you are seeing: the solar wind injects charged particles into Earth's dipole magnetic field; they gyrate around field lines, bounce between magnetic mirror points, and where they dip into the upper atmosphere near the magnetic poles they excite oxygen to glow green (lower aurora) and red (higher aurora). WHAT TO TRY

  • Raise the injection rate: more solar-wind particles spiral down the dipole field lines, bounce between the magnetic mirrors near the poles, and light up the auroral ovals where they hit the atmosphere.
  • Strengthen the dipole: the gyroradius shrinks and particles are trapped more tightly, so the radiation belts and the auroral ring sharpen. The aurora-hit counter tracks the precipitation.
  • Drag the camera around the globe: the trapped population forms the doughnut-shaped Van Allen belts, and the beams funnel into rings around both magnetic poles.
