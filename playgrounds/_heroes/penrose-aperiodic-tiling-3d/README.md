# Penrose Aperiodic Tiling (Hero)

Penrose proved in 1974 that two prototiles (a thick and a thin rhombus)
can tile the plane with matching rules that forbid any periodic
arrangement. The result has local 10-fold symmetry and long-range
order but no translational symmetry; the count ratio of the two
prototiles converges to the golden ratio. Shechtman saw the
3D physical version (Al-Mn quasicrystals) in 1982, winning the 2011
Nobel Prize.

## What to look for

The tiling is drawn as Robinson triangles: cyan are the A halves (of
thick rhombi, 72/108 deg) and orange are the B halves (of thin rhombi,
36/144 deg). Look for the 10-fold rotational symmetry around the
center (the "Sun" patch), the absence of any periodic unit cell, and
the way the star-shaped clusters repeat at multiple scales as a
hallmark of quasiperiodicity.

## Controls

The deflation-steps slider applies the Conway-Penrose substitution
0 to 6 times. Each step multiplies the tile count by $\phi^2 \approx
2.618$. The edge-style toggle hides the per-triangle outlines. The
rotation and zoom sliders are for inspection.
