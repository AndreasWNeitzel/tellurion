# Synchrotron Radiation Cone (Hero)

A relativistic electron in a magnetic field $\vec B$ emits its
radiation into a forward cone of half-angle $\sim 1/\gamma$ along
the instantaneous velocity. The observer sees a sharp pulse every
time the cone sweeps the line of sight, and the spectrum peaks at
$\nu_c = (3/2)\gamma^3 \nu_L$. This is the engine of pulsar wind
nebulae, AGN jets, and laboratory synchrotron light sources.

## What to look for

A glowing electron circles in a magnetic field $\vec B$ (purple
arrows). The orange cone tracks the instantaneous velocity vector;
its half-angle is the famous $1/\gamma$ beaming. The cyan observer
icon on the right receives the radiation. The bottom-left panel
plots the observed pulse $I(\phi)$ as a function of orbital phase:
sharp Gaussian peak of width $\sim 1/\gamma$. The bottom-right panel
shows the spectrum $F(\nu/\nu_c)$ with the canonical $\nu^{1/3}$
rise and exponential cutoff.

## Controls

Slide $\log_{10}\gamma$ from 0 to 5 (non-relativistic to ultra-rel)
and watch the cone shrink. Slide $\log_{10} B$ from $-6$ to $+6$
(microgauss to magnetar field). The view-tilt slider rotates the
orbital plane for inspection. Animation speed scales the orbit
frequency.
