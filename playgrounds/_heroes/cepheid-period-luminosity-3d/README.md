# Cepheid Period-Luminosity (Hero)

Henrietta Leavitt discovered in 1908 that Cepheid variable stars in
the Small Magellanic Cloud have apparent magnitudes correlated with
their pulsation periods. This Leavitt Law, calibrated to absolute
magnitude as $M_V = -2.78 \log_{10} P_{\rm days} - 1.35$, is the
foundational rung of the cosmic distance ladder and anchors the
Type Ia supernova zero point that gives us $H_0$.

## What to look for

Three panels: the pulsating Cepheid (left) is drawn as a sphere whose
radius and surface color (set by $T_{\rm eff}$) cycle with the
pulsation phase. The lightcurve (middle) shows the characteristic
asymmetric Cepheid V-band variability over two periods, with a phase
marker. The Leavitt PL diagram (right) plots the Madore-Freedman 1991
line with five Galactic calibrators (delta Cep, eta Aql, zeta Gem,
l Car, RS Pup) and the currently selected star.

## Controls

The period slider runs from 1 to 80 days, spanning short to long
Cepheids. The preset menu jumps to the named calibrators. The
distance slider controls the assumed line-of-sight distance (in
parsecs) which sets the apparent magnitude via $m_V - M_V = 5\log d -
5$. The animation-speed slider sets the on-screen pulsation rate.
