# Kelvin-Helmholtz Instability (Hero)

A velocity shear between two fluid layers is linearly unstable: the
small-amplitude perturbation grows as $\exp(\sigma t)$ with $\sigma =
kU/2$, and the nonlinear stage rolls up into a row of coherent
vortices spaced one wavelength apart. Stuart (1967) gave the exact
streamfunction $\psi = -\ln(\cosh y + A \cos x)$ that captures the
mature cat's-eye pattern.

## What to look for

Two layers of tracer particles slide past one another at the
centerline (yellow above, cyan below). Streamlines (faint blue) curve
into closed orbits inside the vortex region and pass through above
and below it. As the Stuart amplitude $A$ slider sweeps from 0 (flat
shear) toward 0.7 (sharp vortex cores), the cyan/yellow tracers
spiral together at the vortex center and the interface rolls up.

## Controls

- Stuart $A$ slider (0 to 0.7); $A \to 1$ gives singularly
  concentrated vortices.
- Tracer count (500 to 4000).
- Animation speed.
- Streamlines toggle.

## Source
