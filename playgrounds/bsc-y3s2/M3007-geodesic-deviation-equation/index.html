Geodesic deviation and curvature

What you are seeing: a bundle of geodesics that all start parallel. On a curved surface they do not stay parallel: the normal separation between neighbours obeys the Jacobi equation of geodesic deviation, $\ddot{\xi} + K\,\xi = 0$, where $K$ is the Gaussian curvature. Positive curvature (a sphere) refocuses the bundle to a conjugate point; zero curvature keeps it parallel; negative curvature (a hyperbolic surface) makes it diverge exponentially. Source: Carroll, Spacetime and Geometry, Ch. 3.

WHAT TO TRY

  • Switch the surface between positive, zero, and negative curvature, and watch the same bundle refocus, stay parallel, or diverge.
  • On the sphere, find the conjugate point where every geodesic in the bundle refocuses to a single spot.
  • Change the initial spacing: the normalised separation f(s) is unchanged, because geodesic deviation is linear in the starting offset.
