Hydrogen orbitals in the (x, z) plane

What you are seeing: the probability density $|\psi_{nlm}|^2$ for an electron in a hydrogen atom, sliced through the plane that contains the nucleus and the $z$ axis (so we see the radial nodes and the angular lobes at once). Each panel is a different bound state labeled by three quantum numbers $(n, \ell, m)$: $n$ sets the size and the number of radial nodes, $\ell$ sets the angular shape (s, p, d, f), and $m$ picks one of the $2\ell + 1$ orientations.

The radial part is $R_{n\ell}(r) \propto \rho^\ell\,e^{-r/n}\, L^{2\ell + 1}_{n - \ell - 1}(\rho)$ with $\rho = 2 r / n$; the angular part is a real combination of spherical harmonics $Y^\ell_m(\theta, \phi)$. Standard textbook coordinates, in Bohr radii $a_0$ (where 1 unit = the size of the 1s ground state).
