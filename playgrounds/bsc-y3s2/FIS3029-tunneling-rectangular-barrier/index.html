Quantum tunneling through a rectangular barrier

What you are seeing: a quantum particle with energy $E$ approaches a wall of height $V_0$ and width $a$. Classically, if $E < V_0$ the particle bounces back every time (the dashed grey step). Quantum mechanically it has a non-zero chance $T(E)$ of leaking through, even when $E$ is below $V_0$. Above the barrier the transmission is mostly $1$ but with resonance dips: oscillations from interference inside the barrier.

The blue curve is the analytic transmission probability $T(E)$ for the rectangular barrier; the red ticks are the perfect-transmission resonance energies $E_n = V_0 + n^2 \pi^2 / (2 a^2)$ where $T(E_n) = 1$ exactly. The smaller panel underneath is an animation of the wave at the current energy $E^\star$, with the barrier shaded in red. Watch the wave amplitude inside the barrier: it decays exponentially for $E^\star < V_0$ and oscillates for $E^\star > V_0$. Drag $E^\star$ to sweep through the regimes.
