Bernoulli and the Venturi Effect

Steady incompressible flow through a pipe with a constriction. Mass conservation forces Q=A(x) v(x)=constQ = A(x)\,v(x) = \text{const}Q=A(x)v(x)=const, so the fluid speeds up where the pipe narrows; Bernoulli's theorem p+12ρv2=constp + \tfrac12\rho v^2 = \text{const}p+21​ρv2=const (horizontal pipe) then forces the static pressure down exactly where the speed is up. The piezometer columns make this visible: they are tall in the wide sections and dip at the throat, the Venturi effect. Tracer particles accelerate through the throat (v∝1/Av \propto 1/Av∝1/A). The same principle is the cartoon airfoil: faster flow over the curved top means lower pressure and net upward lift.
