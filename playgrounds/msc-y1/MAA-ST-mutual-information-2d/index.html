Mutual information of a bivariate Gaussian

What you are seeing: the joint probability density of two correlated Gaussian random variables XXX and YYY. The heatmap is p(x,y)p(x, y)p(x,y). The curves on the top and right are the marginals p(x)p(x)p(x) and p(y)p(y)p(y). The number I(X;Y)I(X; Y)I(X;Y) in the corner measures how much knowing XXX reduces your uncertainty about YYY. It is zero when XXX and YYY are independent and grows without bound as they become perfectly correlated.

For a 2D Gaussian with correlation ρ\rhoρ there is a clean closed form: I(X;Y)=−12 ln⁡(1−ρ2).I(X; Y) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\,\ln(1 - \rho^2).I(X;Y)=−21​ln(1−ρ2). Slide ρ\rhoρ to see how the heatmap stretches along the diagonal and how III rises. We compute the same quantity numerically by trapezoidal integration on the same grid and report both numbers; they agree to a few percent at the default resolution.
