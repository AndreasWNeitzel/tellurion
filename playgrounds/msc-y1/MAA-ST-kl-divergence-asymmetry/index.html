KL divergence asymmetry: mass-covering vs mode-seeking

What you are seeing: two probability distributions on the same axis. The thick blue curve is a fixed bimodal target PPP (a mixture of two Gaussians at ±2\pm 2±2). The orange curve is a unimodal approximation QQQ (a single Gaussian) that you control.

KL divergence is not symmetric: D(P∥Q)=∫p(x) log⁡p(x)q(x) dx≠D(Q∥P).D(P \| Q) = \int p(x)\,\log \frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\,dx \neq D(Q \| P).D(P∥Q)=∫p(x)logq(x)p(x)​dx=D(Q∥P). Minimizing D(P∥Q)D(P \| Q)D(P∥Q) over QQQ ("forward" KL, also called M-step KL or "moment matching") gives a Q that covers all the mass of P, even if that means putting probability between the two modes where P has none. Minimizing D(Q∥P)D(Q \| P)D(Q∥P) (reverse KL, used by standard variational inference) gives a Q that collapses onto a single mode: it ignores the other mode entirely. The two "optimal" Q's are very different.
