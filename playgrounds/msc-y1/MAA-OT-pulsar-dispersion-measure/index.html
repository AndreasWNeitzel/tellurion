Pulsar Dispersion Measure Dedispersion

What you are seeing: a spinning neutron star whose radio beams sweep past Earth once per rotation. Each flash crosses the ionized interstellar medium, where lower frequencies travel slower, so it arrives smeared along the $f^{-2}$ dispersion sweep $\Delta t = \mathrm{DM}/(2.41\times10^{-4})\,(1/f_\mathrm{MHz}^2 - 1/f_\mathrm{ref}^2)$ ms. Tune the trial DM to shear the sweep upright; the de-dispersed channel sum then snaps into a sharp spike and the S/N peaks at the true DM. DM is both a nuisance to remove and a measurement: the free-electron column to the source. Reference: Lorimer and Kramer, Handbook of Pulsar Astronomy, Ch. 4.
