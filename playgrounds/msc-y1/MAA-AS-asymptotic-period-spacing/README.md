# Asymptotic period spacing in red giants

A non-radial gravity-mode (g-mode) bounces between two turning radii
inside a star's radiative interior. In the high-radial-order limit
consecutive g-modes of fixed degree ℓ become evenly spaced not in
frequency but in period, with characteristic spacing Π_ℓ set by the
buoyancy structure of the deep interior. The playground renders a
vertical cross-section of the star, animates the radial eigenfunction
ξ_n(r) of a selected g-mode multiplied by the angular dipole or
quadrupole, and reads off the resulting Π_1 (which is what a real
asteroseismic spectrum measures).

Red giants on the red-giant branch (RGB) have an inert helium core
surrounded by a hydrogen-burning shell, which produces a sharp
buoyancy peak deep in the core and a small Π_1 ~ 80 s. After the
helium flash the star ignites helium core burning (the "red clump",
RC); the convective core punches a hole in the g-mode cavity, lowers
the buoyancy integral, and raises Π_1 to ~ 250 s. Switching between
the two presets shows the dramatic difference in interior structure
between two stars that look identical from the surface.

The eigenfunction shape, the period comb, and the evolutionary-state
classification follow Aerts, Christensen-Dalsgaard and Kurtz,
Asteroseismology, Ch. 3, and Bedding et al., Nature 471 (2011) 608,
which established the Π_1 diagnostic as the cleanest seismic separator
of RGB and red-clump giants in Kepler data.
