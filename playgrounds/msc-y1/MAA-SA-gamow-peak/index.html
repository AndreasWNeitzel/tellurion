The Gamow Peak

What you are seeing: two nuclei fuse only if a thermal collision is energetic enough to tunnel through their Coulomb barrier. The number of ions at energy $E$ falls as $e^{-E/kT}$, while the tunneling probability rises as $e^{-\sqrt{E_G/E}}$. Their product is the sharp Gamow peak at $E_0$, which sits far out on the Maxwell-Boltzmann tail in a window where both factors are minuscule. That is why stellar burning rates are so steeply temperature-dependent.

WHAT TO TRY

  • Watch the temperature sweep: the Gamow peak $E_0$ walks to higher energy and the rate climbs by many decades for a small change in $T$.
  • Switch from p + p to ¹²C + ¹²C: the larger nuclear charges raise the Coulomb barrier, push $E_0$ to MeV energies, and demand far higher temperatures to ignite.
  • Read the peak-fraction $I(E_0)$ in the header: even at the optimum energy only a tiny fraction of collisions fuse, yet that is what powers the star.
