# The Gamow peak

The narrow energy window where stellar fusion actually happens. The top panel plots, on a shared energy axis, the falling Maxwell-Boltzmann factor $e^{-E/kT}$, the rising tunneling probability $e^{-\sqrt{E_G/E}}$, and their product, the Gamow peak at $E_0=(\sqrt{E_G}\,kT/2)^{2/3}$, with its Gaussian $1/e$ width $\Delta$ and the true peak fraction $I(E_0)$.

The temperature auto-sweeps so the peak walks to higher energy and grows by orders of magnitude. The bottom panel is the integrated rate against temperature, a steep near-exponential whose local slope $\nu=d\ln(\text{rate})/d\ln T$ is the origin of the stellar thermostat. Switching the reaction selector changes the nuclear charges, lifts the Coulomb barrier, and moves the whole picture to higher energy and temperature.

Controls: the reaction selector picks the colliding pair (p+p through $^{12}$C+$^{12}$C); the temperature slider drives both panels and pauses the sweep; Reset returns to p+p. Source: Clayton, *Principles of Stellar Evolution and Nucleosynthesis* (1983), Sec. 4-3 (`clayton`).
