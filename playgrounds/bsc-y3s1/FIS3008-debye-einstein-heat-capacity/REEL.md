## Beat 2, the reveal (3 to 10s)
VO: Classical physics predicts that every solid has the same molar heat capacity, $3Nk$, the law of Dulong and Petit, and at room temperature most do.
Caption: what you are seeing

## Beat 3, the mechanism (10 to 22s)
VO: But cool a crystal toward absolute zero and its heat capacity collapses, something classical equipartition cannot explain. Einstein gave the first quantum account by treating every atom as an independent oscillator of a single frequency: the modes freeze out when $kT$ drops below $\hbar\omega_E$, and the heat capacity falls.
Caption: the physics, simply

## Beat 4, try it (22 to 33s)
VO: Raise the temperature $T$: the $kT$ line sweeps right, more modes turn warm (active), the lattice jiggles harder, and $C/3Nk$ climbs toward the Dulong-Petit value of 1.
VO: Drop $T$ low: the Einstein spike freezes out all at once (its single frequency needs $kT \gtrsim \hbar\omega_E$), while the Debye continuum keeps a few low-frequency modes active, giving the $T^3$ tail.
Caption: your turn

## Beat 5, payoff and CTA (33 to 40s)
VO: The lower panel puts them on log-log axes, where the Debye curve becomes a straight line of slope three (the $T^3$ law) and the Einstein curve bends sharply away below it: the same data that told physicists in 1912 which model nature actually uses.
