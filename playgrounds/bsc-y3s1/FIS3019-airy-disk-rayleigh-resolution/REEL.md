# Reel script: Airy Disks and the Rayleigh Limit

Vertical 9:16, about 30 to 40 seconds. Screen-record the playground in portrait (820x1040), voiceover plus on-screen captions. Voice: first person, direct, no hype. No em-dashes, no emoji.

## Beat 1, hook (0 to 3s)
VO: Is that one star or two? With a small telescope you genuinely cannot tell. Watch what a bigger mirror does.
Caption: one star, or two?

## Beat 2, the blob (3 to 11s)
VO: A perfect lens cannot focus a star to a point. Diffraction smears it into a bright disk with faint rings, the Airy pattern. Two close stars overlap into a single fuzzy blob.
Caption: every star is a fuzzy disk

## Beat 3, growing the aperture (11 to 22s)
VO: Now grow the aperture. The disks shrink, and at a precise separation the blob splits into two. That threshold is the Rayleigh limit, one point two two times wavelength over diameter.
Caption: bigger aperture, sharper disks

## Beat 4, the dip (22 to 31s)
VO: The bottom curve tells the story: two humps with a dip between them. Once the dip drops to about three quarters of the peak, the pair is resolved. The label flips to RESOLVED.
Caption: the dip is the test

## Beat 5, why it matters and CTA (31 to 40s)
VO: This single formula sets the limit of every telescope, microscope, and eye, and it is why we keep building bigger mirrors. Full interactive version at tellurion.dev. Follow for one of these a day.
Caption: tellurion.dev
