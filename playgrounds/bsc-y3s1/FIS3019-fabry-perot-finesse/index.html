Fabry-Perot etalon: Airy transmission

What you are seeing: a scanned Fabry-Perot cavity. An input beam undergoes multiple partial reflections between two mirrors of reflectance $R$; as the round-trip phase $\phi = 4\pi n L \cos\theta / \lambda$ is swept, the circulating field builds up and the cavity flashes bright with a strong transmitted beam at each resonance ($\phi = 2\pi m$), while between resonances it is dark and the light is reflected.

Raising $R$ sharpens the resonances: the finesse $F_* = \pi\sqrt{R}/(1-R)$ counts FWHM per free spectral range, so $R \approx 0.99$ gives razor-thin peaks ($F_* \approx 313$, almost always missed) and $R = 0.5$ broad ones ($F_* \approx 4.4$). A synced Airy strip plots $T(\phi)$ over three FSRs with a marker tracking the scan, plus the live finesse and $T_{\min}$.
