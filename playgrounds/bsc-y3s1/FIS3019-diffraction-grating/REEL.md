## Beat 2, the reveal (3 to 10s)
VO: A grating is a comb of many fine slits, and shining light through it sharpens the gentle fringes of two slits into a row of needle-thin bright orders, the basis of nearly every spectrometer.
Caption: what you are seeing

## Beat 3, the mechanism (10 to 22s)
VO: The intensity is a product of two patterns: the broad single-slit envelope $(\sin\beta/\beta)^2$ from each slit's own width, multiplied by the many-slit interference factor that spikes wherever all $N$ slits radiate exactly in phase. That happens at the grating equation $d\sin\theta = m\lambda$, the principal maxima, the same condition as two slits but now reinforced by $N$ of them.
Caption: the physics, simply

## Beat 4, try it (22 to 33s)
VO: Raise the slit count $N$: the principal orders knife in, their width shrinking like $1/N$, and the secondary maxima between them ($N-2$ of them) fade.
VO: Change the spacing $d$: the orders crowd together or spread apart, satisfying $d\sin\theta = m\lambda$.
Caption: your turn

## Beat 5, payoff and CTA (33 to 40s)
VO: Slide the slit count, the spacing, and the wavelength, and watch the orders march, spread, and shift in colour.
VO: Full interactive version at tellurion.dev. Follow for one of these a day.
Caption: tellurion.dev
