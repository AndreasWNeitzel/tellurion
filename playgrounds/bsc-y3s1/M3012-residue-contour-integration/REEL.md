# Reel script: The Residue Theorem

Vertical 9:16, about 30 to 40 seconds. Screen-record the playground in portrait (820x1040), voiceover plus on-screen captions. Voice: first person, direct, no hype.

## Beat 1, hook (0 to 3s)
VO: A contour integral of an analytic function ignores the path and counts only the poles inside.
Caption: A contour integral of an analytic functio…

## Beat 2, the reveal (3 to 10s)
VO: Integrating a complex function around a closed loop sounds like it should depend on every twist of the path, but for an analytic function it depends on almost nothing: only on which poles the loop happens to encircle.
Caption: what you are seeing

## Beat 3, the mechanism (10 to 22s)
VO: That is the residue theorem, $\oint_C f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i \sum \mathrm{Res}(f, z_k)$ summed over the poles inside $C$, and it is the workhorse that turns hopeless real integrals into a quick tally of residues. The scene colours the plane by the phase of $f$, the hue cycling through every value around each pole so the singularities glow as little pinwheels, and marks each pole with its residue.
Caption: the physics, simply

## Beat 4, try it (22 to 33s)
VO: Grow the radius from zero: the integral is flat, then jumps by $2\pi i\,\mathrm{Res}$ the instant the loop swallows a pole (the staircase below).
VO: Drag the contour so it encloses no pole: the integral collapses to zero, whatever the path.
Caption: your turn

## Beat 5, payoff and CTA (33 to 40s)
VO: Cycle the functions to watch the staircase rearrange itself around poles on the real axis, on the imaginary axis, and at the origin.
VO: Full interactive version at tellurion.dev. Follow for one of these a day.
Caption: tellurion.dev
