# Reel script: The Maxwell-Boltzmann Speed Distribution

Vertical 9:16, about 30 to 40 seconds. Screen-record the playground in portrait (820x1040), voiceover plus on-screen captions. Voice: first person, direct, no hype.

## Beat 1, hook (0 to 3s)
VO: A gas at one temperature is a riot of speeds.
Caption: A gas at one temperature is a riot of spe…

## Beat 2, the reveal (3 to 10s)
VO: A gas at a single temperature is not a crowd of molecules all moving alike; it is a riot of speeds, some crawling, some tearing along, most somewhere in the middle.
Caption: what you are seeing

## Beat 3, the mechanism (10 to 22s)
VO: The spread is not arbitrary. Because each component of a molecule's velocity is an independent Gaussian set by the temperature, the speed, which is the length of that velocity vector, follows the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution $f(v) \propto v^2 e^{-mv^2/2kT}$.
Caption: the physics, simply

## Beat 4, try it (22 to 33s)
VO: Watch the histogram fill in the smooth $f(v)$ curve as more speeds are sampled, peaking at the most probable speed.
VO: Raise the temperature: the distribution slides right and flattens, the molecules in the box turning redder, but the area stays one.
Caption: your turn

## Beat 5, payoff and CTA (33 to 40s)
VO: Turn the temperature up and the whole distribution slides to higher speeds and flattens out, keeping its area at one; make the molecules heavier and it shrinks back toward the slow end, since at the same temperature a heavier molecule moves more sedately.
VO: Full interactive version at tellurion.dev. Follow for one of these a day.
