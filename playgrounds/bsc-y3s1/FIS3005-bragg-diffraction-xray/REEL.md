# Reel script: X-ray Bragg Diffraction

Vertical 9:16, about 30 to 40 seconds. Screen-record the playground in portrait (820x1040), voiceover plus on-screen captions. Voice: first person, direct, no hype.

## Beat 1, hook (0 to 3s)
VO: Why does a crystal reflect X-rays only at special angles?
Caption: Why does a crystal reflect X-rays only at…

## Beat 2, the reveal (3 to 10s)
VO: Shine X-rays of wavelength comparable to the atomic spacing at a crystal, and most angles give nothing, but at a few sharp angles a strong reflected beam appears.
Caption: what you are seeing

## Beat 3, the mechanism (10 to 22s)
VO: Bragg explained it with a picture so simple it earned a Nobel Prize: treat the crystal as a stack of parallel planes of atoms, each reflecting a little of the wave. A ray that reflects off the second plane travels an extra distance compared to one reflecting off the first, and a short construction shows that extra path is exactly $2d\sin\theta$, where $d$ is the plane spacing and $\theta$ the glancing angle.
Caption: the physics, simply

## Beat 4, try it (22 to 33s)
VO: Sweep the angle: the reflected beam flares bright each time the path difference passes a whole number of wavelengths.
VO: Read the path-difference label: at a peak it is exactly $n\lambda$, and the order $n$ is named.
Caption: your turn

## Beat 5, payoff and CTA (33 to 40s)
VO: Sweep the angle to walk through the peaks, or change the spacing and wavelength and watch the whole pattern shift, the inverse relationship that lets a diffraction pattern be read back as a map of the crystal.
VO: Full interactive version at tellurion.dev. Follow for one of these a day.
Caption: tellurion.dev
