Gauss's law: flux is invariant under deformation

What you are seeing: a point charge qqq inside (or outside) a deformable closed 2D loop. The flux of the electric field through the loop is computed numerically by Simpson quadrature on ∮E⋅n^ ds\oint \mathbf{E} \cdot \hat n \, ds∮E⋅n^ds. For 2D Gauss the result is q/ϵ0q / \epsilon_0q/ϵ0​ whenever the charge sits inside, and zero when it sits outside, no matter how the loop is deformed.

Slide the ellipse semi-axes or switch to the blob shape. The readout reports the computed flux and the ratio to the theoretical value q/ϵ0q / \epsilon_0q/ϵ0​. Move the charge position out past the ellipse boundary and the flux drops to zero.
