Doppler effect from a moving source

What you are seeing: a source moving at constant velocity vvv to the right (cyan dot) emits a circular wavefront once per source-frame period T=1/fT = 1 / fT=1/f. Each wavefront then expands at the wave speed ccc. Because the source moves between emissions, the wavefronts cluster in front of the source and stretch behind it. The coloured bar along the source axis measures the wavelength directly: a short blue λfront=(c−v)/f\lambda_\text{front} = (c - v)/fλfront​=(c−v)/f ahead and a long warm λback=(c+v)/f\lambda_\text{back} = (c + v)/fλback​=(c+v)/f behind. The source loops: when it leaves the right edge it re-enters from the left and the pattern continues.

Pick any angle θ\thetaθ from the velocity vector. A stationary observer there hears frequency fobs=f/(1−(v/c)cos⁡θ)f_\text{obs} = f / (1 - (v/c) \cos\theta)fobs​=f/(1−(v/c)cosθ).
