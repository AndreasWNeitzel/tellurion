Triangular-lattice Ising model

What you are seeing: a grid of tiny magnets (spins) on a triangular lattice. Each one points up (+1, blue) or down (-1, red). Neighboring spins prefer to align (lower energy when they agree). Crank the temperature $T$ up and thermal noise wins: spins flip randomly, the lattice looks like static. Drop $T$ down and the magnets line up into one giant domain. Right at the critical temperature $T_c = 4 / \ln 3 \approx 3.641$ (Wannier 1950, exact) the system is on the knife edge: domains of every size appear simultaneously.

The simulation is Metropolis single-spin flips with periodic boundary conditions. Magnetization $m = \langle s \rangle$ should be zero above $T_c$ and drift toward $\pm 1$ below. Watch the magnetization read-out to see the order-disorder transition in real time.
