Kepler Orbit Explorer

Watch the inner solar system run. Each planet traces an ellipse with the Sun at one focus, and the further out it orbits, the slower it goes, not a little slower, but on a precise schedule: the orbital period squared is proportional to the orbit size cubed. Mercury laps the Sun four times before Earth completes one circuit; Mars takes nearly two Earth years. This is Kepler's third law, and it falls straight out of Newtonian gravity. Along the way each planet also speeds up at its closest approach and dawdles at its farthest, sweeping equal areas in equal times. The bodies here move under the real inverse-square force, integrated with an energy-conserving symplectic scheme, with the actual eccentricities of the planets. Drop a comet at any distance and watch where it lands on the period-versus-size law. The scene is the orbiting system; the diagnostic is the third law itself, every body falling on one straight line.

WHAT TO TRY

  • Watch Mercury whip around several times while Mars barely moves: the outer orbits really are slower.
  • Follow one planet near the Sun and far from it: it visibly races at perihelion, crawls at aphelion (equal areas).
  • Slide the comet outward and watch its point climb the period-size line in the lower plot, always on it.
  • Notice the comet's stretched, eccentric orbit compared with the nearly circular planets.
