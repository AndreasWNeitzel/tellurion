# Reel script: The HR Diagram: Real Stars and a Stellar Evolution Track

Vertical 9:16, about 30 to 40 seconds. Screen-record the playground in portrait (820x1040), voiceover plus on-screen captions. Voice: first person, direct, no hype.

## Beat 1, hook (0 to 3s)
VO: Plot 4000 real Gaia stars on the HR diagram and thread a real stellar-evolution model through them: a Sun-like star creeps along the main sequence for nine billion years, then races through the giant phases.
Caption: Plot 4000 real Gaia stars on the HR diagr…

## Beat 2, the reveal (3 to 10s)
VO: The Hertzsprung-Russell diagram is the single most important plot in stellar astrophysics: where a star sits on it is set by its temperature and luminosity, and a star traces a path across it as it ages.
Caption: what you are seeing

## Beat 3, the mechanism (10 to 22s)
VO: This playground plots 4000 real Gaia DR3 stars on the Kiel plane (effective temperature against surface gravity), and threads a real MESA stellar-evolution model for a Sun-like star straight through them. Drag the age and watch a marker walk the model track from the zero-age main sequence, through the subgiant branch and up the red giant branch, through helium burning and the asymptotic giant branch, and finally down to the white-dwarf graveyard.
Caption: the physics, simply

## Beat 4, try it (22 to 33s)
VO: Play or drag the evolution slider: it steps by distance travelled in the diagram, not by age, so the subgiant and giant phases (under 1 Gyr of real time) finally get their share of screen time instead of flashing past. The age readout still shows the true age, which races ahead once the star leaves the main sequence.
VO: Compare the orange 1 Msun model with the violet Gaia ridge (the running median of the real stars): on the main sequence the model runs hotter than the ridge, because a volume-limited local sample is dominated by cool, low-mass M dwarfs far more numerous than Sun-like stars. Find the green turn-off circle where core hydrogen runs out (about 9 Gyr).
Caption: your turn

## Beat 5, payoff and CTA (33 to 40s)
VO: The Gaia sample here is volume-limited within 100 parsecs, an unbiased local census selected on proximity and parallax quality rather than brightness, so it is dominated by faint, low-mass main-sequence stars exactly as the lifetimes predict, and luminous giants are rare because no nearby star is caught in that brief phase right now.
VO: Full interactive version at tellurion.dev. Follow for one of these a day.
Caption: tellurion.dev
