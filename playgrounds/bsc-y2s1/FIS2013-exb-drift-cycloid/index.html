E x B drift and the cycloid

What you are seeing: a charged particle in crossed uniform fields, B=Bz^\mathbf{B} = B \hat{z}B=Bz^ (out of page) and E=Ex^\mathbf{E} = E \hat{x}E=Ex^ (right). Starting from rest, the particle traces a cycloid: it accelerates in the +x+x+x direction under E\mathbf{E}E until the resulting v×B\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B}v×B curves it back, then repeats. The net motion is a uniform drift in the E×B/B2\mathbf{E} \times \mathbf{B} / B^2E×B/B2 direction (here −y-y−y).

The drift velocity vd=E×B/B2\mathbf{v}_d = \mathbf{E} \times \mathbf{B} / B^2vd​=E×B/B2 is independent of the particle's charge and mass; positive and negative particles drift the same way. The cycloid amplitude is E/B2E / B^2E/B2 and its period equals the cyclotron period Tc=2πm/(qB)T_c = 2 \pi m / (q B)Tc​=2πm/(qB). Vary EEE to make the drift faster; vary BBB to tighten the cycloid loops.
