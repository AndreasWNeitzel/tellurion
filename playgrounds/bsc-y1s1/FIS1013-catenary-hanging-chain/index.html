Catenary: shape of a hanging chain

What you are seeing: a uniform, perfectly flexible cable hung as a suspension bridge between two towers. Carrying only its own weight it takes the catenary y(x)=acosh⁡(x/a)−ay(x) = a \cosh(x / a) - ay(x)=acosh(x/a)−a, where a=T0/(μg)a = T_0 / (\mu g)a=T0​/(μg) is the catenary parameter (horizontal tension T0T_0T0​, linear mass density μ\muμ, gravity ggg). A small aaa is a deep, slack, low-tension sag; a large aaa is a shallow, taut, nearly straight cable. The readout shows aaa, the cable length, span, sag and the peak tension (largest at the supports).

Drag either tower: the cable length is fixed, so the catenary re-solves through the new supports. Pull the towers far enough apart that the cable cannot reach and it snaps taut to a straight line. A shallow cable looks almost parabolic, y≈x2/2ay \approx x^2 / 2ay≈x2/2a (the classical suspension-bridge approximation), but the computed shape is always the exact cosh, which grows faster than that quadratic for deep sag.
