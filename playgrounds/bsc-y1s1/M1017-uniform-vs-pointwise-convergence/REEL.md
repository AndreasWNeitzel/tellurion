# Reel script: Uniform vs Pointwise Convergence

Vertical 9:16, about 30 to 40 seconds. Screen-record the playground in portrait (820x1040), voiceover plus on-screen captions. Voice: first person, direct, no hype.

## Beat 1, hook (0 to 3s)
VO: f_n can settle at every single point yet never settle as a whole.
Caption: f_n can settle at every single point yet…

## Beat 2, the reveal (3 to 10s)
VO: A sequence of functions can converge in two quite different senses, and the gap between them is where a lot of analysis lives.
Caption: what you are seeing

## Beat 3, the mechanism (10 to 22s)
VO: Pointwise convergence is the modest claim that at each fixed $x$ the numbers $f_n(x)$ settle to a limit $f(x)$, each point minding its own business. Uniform convergence is the demanding claim that the entire graph settles together, that the worst-case vertical gap between $f_n$ and $f$, the sup-norm $\lVert f_n - f\rVert_\infty$, shrinks to zero.
Caption: the physics, simply

## Beat 4, try it (22 to 33s)
VO: Watch the sweep: for the ramp the whole curve flattens onto zero (uniform), but for the bumps a spike slides to the edge and the gap stays open.
VO: On $x^n$, see the limit turn discontinuous, a jump at $x=1$ that no continuous uniform limit could produce.
Caption: your turn

## Beat 5, payoff and CTA (33 to 40s)
VO: The bottom panel plots that sup-norm against $n$, the one number that tells uniform from merely pointwise, and the draggable point on top tracks $f_n(x_0)$ settling pointwise even when the sup-norm will not.
VO: Full interactive version at tellurion.dev. Follow for one of these a day.
Caption: tellurion.dev
