Foucault pendulum: Coriolis precession

What you are seeing: a small-amplitude pendulum swinging in the horizontal $(x, y)$ plane on a rotating Earth. The Coriolis force $2 \Omega \sin(\phi) \, \hat{z} \times \mathbf{v}$ does no work, but it causes the plane of oscillation to slowly rotate. The trace pattern is a Spirograph-like rosette: the pendulum still oscillates, but the long axis of each swing rotates between swings.

The precession period is $T_{\text{precess}} = T_0 / \sin(\phi)$ where $T_0$ is the rotation period at the pole. At the equator there is no Coriolis component along the vertical, so the swing plane does not rotate. In the southern hemisphere the rotation reverses direction. Time is scaled here so $T_0$ is 24 seconds (not 24 hours) for visibility. Try the latitude slider to move from the equator to a pole.
