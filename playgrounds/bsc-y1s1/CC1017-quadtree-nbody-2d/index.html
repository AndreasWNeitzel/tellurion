Quadtree N-body and the Barnes-Hut approximation

What you are seeing: a 2D gravity-bound disk of N test particles around a heavy central mass. The brown squares are the live quadtree partitioning that adapts to the moving distribution every step. The opening angle $\theta$ controls when a distant cluster of bodies is approximated as a single point at its centre of mass. Toggle the tree off to see direct $O(N^2)$ summation, and watch the per-step evaluation count jump in the readout. Source: Barnes and Hut, Nature 324 (1986) 446 (barnes-hut1986).
