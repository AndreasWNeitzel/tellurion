## Beat 2, the reveal (3 to 10s)
VO: Spin a phone, a book, or a tennis racket about its three axes and one of them misbehaves.
Caption: what you are seeing

## Beat 3, the mechanism (10 to 22s)
VO: A free rigid body has three principal axes, and Euler's equations make spin about the largest and smallest moments of inertia stable, but spin about the middle one unstable: the slightest wobble grows until the body suddenly flips end over end, then flips back, over and over. This is the tennis-racket theorem, the same Dzhanibekov effect a cosmonaut filmed a wing-nut doing in orbit.
Caption: the physics, simply

## Beat 4, try it (22 to 33s)
VO: Keep the spin axis on middle and watch the body flip end over end, again and again, with no force applied.
VO: Switch to the major or minor axis: now it spins smoothly and never flips, those axes are stable.
Caption: your turn

VO: The scene tumbles the body under Euler's equations; the diagnostic plots the three body-frame spin rates, where the middle one periodically reverses sign, each reversal a flip.
