Catastrophic cancellation in floating point

What you are seeing: the relative error of the naive formula $1 - \cos(x)$ as $x \to 0$ on a log-log plot, compared to the algebraically identical but numerically stable $2 \sin^2(x/2)$. For small $x$, $\cos(x)$ is very close to 1, and subtracting them loses most of the significant digits in IEEE-754 double precision. The fix uses the half-angle identity to compute the small quantity directly, never forming the near-equal difference.

The naive curve plummets toward the floor at $x \sim 10^{-8}$ (about $\sqrt{\epsilon_\text{mach}}$) and pegs at 100 percent relative error around $x \sim 10^{-15}$ where it returns exactly zero. The stable curve stays at machine precision throughout.
