Lissajous Figures

What you are seeing: a parametric curve generated by two perpendicular harmonic oscillations, x(t)=Asin⁡(at+δ)x(t) = A \sin(a t + \delta)x(t)=Asin(at+δ) and y(t)=Bsin⁡(bt)y(t) = B \sin(b t)y(t)=Bsin(bt). The shape depends only on the ratio a/ba / ba/b and the phase δ\deltaδ. When a/ba / ba/b is rational, the curve closes after time T=2π/gcd⁡(a,b)T = 2\pi / \gcd(a, b)T=2π/gcd(a,b); when irrational, it never closes and densely fills a bounding box.

The classical 1:1 case with δ=π/2\delta = \pi/2δ=π/2 gives a circle; with δ=0\delta = 0δ=0 it gives the line y=xy = xy=x. The 1:2 ratio gives a figure-eight. As the ratio gets more complex (3:4, 3:5, 5:7), the pattern becomes a denser quasi-grid. Phase δ\deltaδ rotates and re-shapes the figure smoothly.

WHAT TO TRY

  • Watch the wave above and the wave on the left: their current values are the dot's x and y. The figure is just those two oscillations combined.
  • Tap a ratio in the gallery, or set $a$ and $b$ by hand. Simple ratios like 1:2 and 2:3 give clean loops; 5:7 fills the box with a dense mesh.
  • With $a = b$, slide the phase $\delta$ from 0 to $\pi/2$ and watch a diagonal line open into a circle.
