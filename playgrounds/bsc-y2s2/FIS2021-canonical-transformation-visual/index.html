Canonical Transformations

A change of phase-space coordinates (q,p)→(Q,P)(q,p)\to(Q,P)(q,p)→(Q,P) is canonical when it preserves the symplectic structure: the Poisson bracket {Q,P}=∂qQ ∂pP−∂pQ ∂pP=1\{Q,P\}=\partial_qQ\,\partial_pP-\partial_pQ\,\partial_pP=1{Q,P}=∂q​Q∂p​P−∂p​Q∂p​P=1, equivalently the Jacobian is unimodular, equivalently it preserves phase-space area (Liouville). The left panel is a blob of phase points; the right is its image under the chosen map. The harmonic scaling turns the energy ellipse into a circle; a rotation spins it; a squeeze stretches it thin but keeps the area; a deliberately non-canonical ppp-doubling balloons it to twice the area, with {Q,P}=2\{Q,P\}=2{Q,P}=2. A canonical map need not be a symmetry: the squeeze is canonical yet changes the Hamiltonian.

WHAT TO TRY

  • Switch the map: harmonic scaling squashes a circle into an ellipse, a phase rotation spins it, and an area-preserving squeeze stretches one axis while shrinking the other. The grid deforms with the blob.
  • Watch the area ratio: every canonical map keeps it pinned at 1, since the Poisson bracket {Q,P} = 1. That is the symplectic condition, area in phase space is conserved.
  • Pick the p-doubling map, which is NOT canonical: the area ratio jumps away from 1, and the blob inflates. A transformation that changes phase-space area cannot come from any Hamiltonian.
