Photoelectric effect: KE max vs photon frequency

What you are seeing: Einstein's 1905 photoelectric prediction $KE_{max} = h \nu - \phi$ plotted against incident photon frequency $\nu$ for eight metals, each with its own work function $\phi$. Below the threshold $\nu_0 = \phi/h$ no electrons are ejected regardless of light intensity. Above threshold the slope of every line is the same Planck constant $h$, with the metal-specific intercept fixing where the line crosses zero.

Drag the photon frequency slider; the readout shows the ejected electron energy for the highlighted metal. Switching metals slides the threshold along the frequency axis without changing the slope. The implied wavelength threshold is $\lambda_0 = hc/\phi$, so cesium (low work function) responds to visible light, while platinum needs deep UV.
