The Photoelectric Effect

Light of frequency $\nu$ strikes a metal cathode. Einstein's result, $K_{\max} = h\nu - \phi$, says an electron is ejected only if the photon energy exceeds the work function: below the threshold $\nu_0 = \phi/h$ no electrons appear no matter how bright the light, the result classical wave theory could not explain. Raising the intensity adds more electrons but never makes them faster; raising the frequency does. The side panels are the current-voltage curve (cut off at the stopping voltage) and the Einstein line $V_{\rm stop}$ versus $\nu$ with universal slope $h/e$.
