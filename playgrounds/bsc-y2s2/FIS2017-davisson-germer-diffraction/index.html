Davisson-Germer electron diffraction

What you are seeing: the 1927 experiment that confirmed de Broglie's hypothesis. Electrons accelerated through voltage $V$ have de Broglie wavelength $\lambda = h/\sqrt{2 m_e e V}$. When they scatter off a nickel (111) surface with atomic row spacing $D = 0.215$ nm, the angular intensity pattern peaks at $D \sin\theta = n\lambda$. At the original $V = 54$ V the first-order peak sits at $\theta \approx 51^\circ$, matching the measured maximum.

The left panel sketches the geometry: incident electron beam vertical, Ni surface horizontal, the principal-order peak direction shown by the accent-colored arrow. The right panel plots $I(\theta) = (\sin N\phi / \sin\phi)^2$, the standard N-slit grating intensity with $\phi = \pi D \sin\theta / \lambda$. Higher orders appear as additional sharp peaks at larger $\theta$.
